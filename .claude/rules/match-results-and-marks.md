# Match results and marks

Moved from CLAUDE.md "Match results and marks" and "Key Algorithms". Read before changing how a result is recorded, validated, or shown on any surface or in the workbook.

- **Operator match labels** (`internal/engine/match_label.go`): `OperatorMatchLabel(comp, bracket, matchID)` is the ONE Go owner of how a match is named back to the operator -- in refusal messages, the `court_busy`/`downstream_knockout_running` `label` fields, and `reasonHuman` sentences. A pool/league row reads e.g. `"Pool A · Match 1"` (`"League table · Match N"` for a league; the middle dot is the literal separator, `supplementaryOrNumberedLabel`); a knockout row (`MatchLabel`) reads e.g. `"Match 2 (Final)"`, `"knockout Match N"` (no round recorded), or `"the 3rd-place match"` (bronze). Pinned against the JS side by the shared golden fixture `internal/engine/testdata/match_labels.json`: `TestOperatorMatchLabel_GoldenTable` (Go) and `web-mobile/js/__tests__/match_labels_golden.test.jsx` (JS, `scoreRowMatchLabel`/`matchLabel` from `pool_ids.jsx`/`write_result.jsx`) load the SAME file.
- **Match Colors**: White (Shiro) is always the left column and Red (Aka) is always the right column (this is fixed and not configurable). In the Excel export, `helper.WhiteLeft` (`internal/helper/excel.go`) is the one Go owner of that side-to-column order; no mirror setting exists, and every side-ordered pair (entrant formulas and names, scores, fouls, result marks, header labels and styles, standings side records, a team summary's IV/PW) routes through it rather than restating the order. In pool matches the first-listed player (SideA) is Red; in elimination matches the upper-bracket player (`node.Left`) is Red. **A TINTED surface does not carry a SHIRO/AKA badge as well** (operator ruling 2026-09-20, bc-sccl, which SUPERSEDES bc-dnst's "named once in text, by the header badge"): where a side has a background tint, the tint plus Shiro's 45° hatch carry it, and the badge is redundant. Every side badge in the app went with that ruling: the score editors' header pill (`.sb-side__badge`), the engi card's (`.engi-side__badge`), the scores list and court console's (`.se-color-badge`) and the compact schedule's 14×14 `A`/`S` squares (`.tw-match__badge`). Bout rows and foul counters were already tint-only, so do not re-add per-bout SHIRO/AKA chips or "SHIRO Fouls" prefixes either. A tinted surface still names the side in TEXT for a screen reader — that is the one thing that must never be dropped with a badge (DESIGN.md §4: colour is never the only signal). The list of surfaces that carry no such text is short and closed: the TV board and the bracket card (both below, each by its own ruling), plus the watchlist hero, which names it VISIBLY instead. **`SideCell` (side_cell.jsx) owns this: a surface passes `side` and gets the fill class AND the `sr-only` label together, so the tint cannot arrive without its text; `SideLabel` is the label alone, for a surface that paints its own tint; `sideFillClass` is the class alone, for the watchlist hero only, which names the side visibly and so must not carry the span as well.** Do not hand-type either the class or the span -- that is how `"Shiro: "` and `"Shiro:"` came to coexist. **Use an `sr-only` span**, via that owner. An `aria-label` only works on an element whose role permits naming, and the cells this rule governs are bare `<div>`s, i.e. role `generic`, where ARIA PROHIBITS it: the court console carried `aria-label={`Shiro: …`}` on four such cells and the side was announced by nothing. An `aria-label` on an interactive wrapper is worse than useless here, because role=button has presentational children, so it REPLACES the side word, the competitor numbers and the dojos inside it — that is how the watchlist hero lost all of them. ONE exception, and it is about SIZE, not colour: the watchlist hero (`.wl-hero__side-lbl`) keeps a visible `Aka`/`Shiro` word (operator decision 2026-09-20). It is a large personal card read at arm's length, not a dense row, so the word costs it nothing -- which is the whole reason the ruling removed the badge from dense rows. Do not copy it onto a row. The exception this REPLACES was `.bc-color-badge`, justified by that card being dark navy; bc-wlhc made the card light and deleted the class, so the old wording named a surface that no longer existed. Controls that PICK a side (the "SHIRO wins"/"AKA wins" buttons, the decision side picker, the team summary band) keep naming it, as does the OBS lower third's heading. The TV board does NOT: its SHIRO/AKA headings went with the same ruling (position plus each name's colour carry it there, and a projector has no screen-reader audience), pinned by `side_is_carried_by_tint.test.jsx`'s "the TV board names no side". Aria-labels and titles may always say the side. The competitor number sits on the OUTER side of the name on every surface, Shiro's before it and Aka's after (operator ruling 2026-09-14, bc-dnst); `NumberedName` (numbered_name.jsx) owns it for JSX and `withNumber` (match_scoreboard.jsx) for strings, so a new surface calls one of those two and never restates the before/after choice. **Which of the two depends on one thing: does the cell CLIP?** A cell with `text-overflow: ellipsis` truncates the END of its run, which on Aka is the number, so the string form silently loses it there while Shiro's leading number survives. A clipping cell therefore passes `clip` to `NumberedName` (or, on the display surfaces, takes `sideLabelParts` from display_helpers.jsx), which makes the chip its own flex child so only the name ellipsises; a wrapping or unconstrained cell keeps the string. Both forms read the same `numberedParts`, so they cannot disagree about a side's name and number. The OBS lower third is MIXED on exactly this basis, not string-only (bc-rvfx): its team lines clip and chip, its individual lines do not clip and keep the string. The TV board WAS mixed on the same basis and is now chip-only (operator decision 2026-09-19, bc-lbty): its `NextPair` rows (the NEXT line and the UP NEXT pool bout list) still do not clip, but they render the non-clip `NumberedName` anyway, because a chipped row directly above a bare-string row read as an oversight rather than a choice. Clipping still decides `clip` vs plain; it no longer decides chip vs string, so do NOT infer from that exception that every non-clipping cell must chip -- the overlay's individual lines are the standing counter-example. **A chip takes the COLOUR of the name it rides beside** (operator decision 2026-09-19, bc-lbty): `.num-prefix` is `color: inherit` with its tint mixed from `currentColor` toward `transparent`, so Shiro's chip is dark and Aka's is red, and the chip never introduces colour the adjacent name does not already carry. The mix must stay transparency-based, never toward a solid light colour: the OBS lower third paints on `rgba(0,0,0,0.85)` with white and pale-pink text, where a white-mixed chip would be invisible. This supersedes the earlier mp-938b neutral-chip note, which the `.num-prefix` comment in styles.css no longer carries and which survives in git history only. Where the two sides STACK vertically instead of sitting left/right (the bracket card, the admin and public schedule rows) there is no outer side: the number sits before the name on both so the numbers align in a column, and the bracket card names no side in text, its leading colour bar and tint carry it (operator ruling 2026-09-14, bc-dnst).
- **Clearing a scored mark is a tap on the mark, and each editor says so ONCE** (operator ruling 2026-09-16, bc-dnst). The individual board puts the line under its slot groups; the TEAM sheet puts it after the team names and BEFORE the first bout, never per bout, because the sheet repeats a row per bout. Both use the same words and the same `.sb-hint` owner (`.sb-hint--encounter` only adds the spacing a line between two blocks needs), and both show it only while a mark exists to clear. The team sheet keeps the line's space while it is hidden (`.holds-space`, the one owner of `visibility: hidden` for this, operator ruling 2026-10-06), because it sits above every bout row: appearing at a bout's first point (in kachinuki at every bout, since a recorded bout's marks no longer count) it moved every row 28 px down, and a quick second tap on M opened the fighter list. The individual board's line sits below what a scoring tap aims at, so it still comes and goes. A hantei mark does not raise it: `realIppons` drops it and it undoes through its own "click to undo" control.


The rules below were settled by operator rulings and are pinned by tests. Each has one owner in code; do not restate any of them at a call site.

## Decision types

`internal/domain/decision.go` defines the canonical wire values: `""` (none), `"fought"`, `"hikiwake"` (draw), `"kiken"` (legacy withdrawal, maps to kiken-voluntary on YAML load), `"kiken-voluntary"` (FIK Art. 31, permanent), `"kiken-injury"` (FIK Art. 30, reinstateable), `"fusenpai"` (no-show), `"fusensho"` (recorded per bout within a team encounter, AND at match level by an operator via `POST /decision` to close a barred competitor's remaining scheduled match -- `domain.IsWithdrawalDecisionStr` deliberately excludes it, since it is awarded on someone ELSE's withdrawal rather than a withdrawal of the match it is recorded on; `domain.IsDefaultWinDecisionStr` includes it. `decisionBy` on either usage names the side that withdrew, did not appear, or is barred -- the LOSING side -- never the side the decision favours; the write then makes the OTHER side the winner (`scoring_tx.go`)), `"daihyosen"` (rep bout), `"kachinuki-exhaustion"` (kachinuki competitions only: a score write carrying it, at match level or on a bout row, in any other competition is refused by `refuseKachinukiDecision`/`refuseKachinukiDecisionForComp` in `validation.go`, a 400 on `PUT /score` and a per-entry error on bulk-score), and `"ippon-shobu"`. The last one is accepted by `Decision.Valid` but has no writer in the tree: supplementary pool tie-break bouts are identified by their match id (`Pool X-TB-N`, see `engine/tiebreaker.go`), not by this decision. Use `domain.IsKikenDecision(d)` / `domain.IsKikenDecisionStr(s)` to check any kiken variant. Legacy YAML `decision: true` migrates to `"hikiwake"`, `false` to `"fought"`, `"kiken"` to `"kiken-voluntary"` (`Decision.UnmarshalYAML`).

## The middle is a closed set

Visual marks follow the score sheet's layout, split into two kinds. MIDDLE marks occupy the centre "vs" cell (the score-string centre): exactly one of `X` (tie), `(E)` (overtime, always bare: `periodCount` is recorded but never displayed), `(DH)` (rep bout). They are mutually exclusive by rule: a match that went to encho cannot end tied, so X beats (E); a daihyosen is one-point sudden death, so DH bouts have no encho. RESULT marks ride beside the competitor they name: `Ht` (hantei winner), `Kiken` (withdrawer), `Fus.` (no-show, and also the winner of a match-level fusensho; `sideMarks` (side_marks.jsx, re-exported by bracket.jsx) and `domain.SideMarks` (placed on the sheet by `SideMarksLR`, internal/export/suffix.go) agree on this, so a fusensho match reads identically on every surface and in the exported workbook).

Web score strings read `[left cell] [middle] [right cell]` with `vs` as the plain middle and `–` (or empty) meaning no points. Never digits: ippon renders as waza letters only, e.g. `M vs –`, `M (E) K`, `M Ht (E) K`. The dash is a CELL value only. The middle never shows a dash, so unplayed and pending match rows read `vs` too.

A kiken, fusenpai or fusensho awards the WINNER its points as maru, one `○` per point per FIK Regulations Art. 32 and the Score Board appendix p.15 (`["○","○"]` in regulation, one `○` in encho). This is the rulebook's score-board marking, not a house style; it lives in `domain.DefaultWinIppons`. Per the same Article ("Any point scored by the shiai-funo-sha shall remain valid") the LOSER is NOT zeroed: `preserveLoserScore` (engine/scoring.go, called by the one `RecordDecision` body) keeps the withdrawing side's already-struck ippons and preserves the encounter's prior `SubResults`, so a team withdrawal never wipes the sub-bouts already fought (they keep counting in IV/PW standings). It is guarded on a side-matching prior so a drifted prior can't mis-attribute points. `state.EnchoMetadata.On` and `enchoOn` (result_slot.jsx) are the single "did this happen in encho" predicate (non-nil AND positive periodCount), shared by the (E) label, the maru count, and decision validation, so a degenerate `{periodCount: 0}` block can never split the surfaces. Display fallbacks (`DefaultWinMaruAB` in suffix.go, `formatIpponsScore`, the scoreboard win slots) render `○○` for winners whose recorded cells are empty (byes, pre-fill/legacy data). The team editor's per-bout **Fusensho** follows the same rule on the client (bc-fsnp): `applyFusenshoToggle` (admin_scoring_shared.jsx) keeps the other side's points through `struckIppons`, the JS twin of `engine.struckIppons` (result_slot.jsx, beside `DEFAULT_WIN_IPPON`), `fusenshoAllowed` refuses a fusensho against a side that already won the bout (circles against two points would be a 2-2; the refused button stays tappable and shows why under that side until the next bout edit, since a title never shows on a touchscreen), and every scoring edit on a team bout row goes through the editor's `updateSubScore`, whose `applyBoutScoreEdit` ends a fusensho through `clearFusensho` applied LAST, so the circles never survive as ordinary points. One edit keeps it: taking a mark off the side the fusensho went against is a correction, and the snapshot the undo restores follows it.

The middle-value rule lives in ONE place, `boutMiddle` (bracket.jsx), which returns exactly `vs`/`X`/`(E)`/`(DH)`; `matchMiddleMark` (the chip variant), `matchStateCell`, and the scoreboard separators all derive from it. (`formatIpponsScore` composes the same `isDrawResult`/`middleMark` primitives directly rather than calling `boutMiddle`: its empty-middle-vs-`""` distinction is load-bearing for callers' fallbacks.)

**The middle is a CLOSED SET and there must NEVER be a centre `Ht`** (operator ruling, absolute). Those four values are the only things that may ever occupy the centre, in either language and on every surface. `Ht`/`Kiken`/`Fus.` name ONE competitor, so they ride in that competitor's cell and can never take the shared cell. This is enforced, not just documented: `web-mobile/js/__tests__/middle_closed_set.test.jsx` and `internal/export/middle_closed_set_test.go` sweep every decision × encho × score (including `decidedByHantei`) and fail on a fifth value OR a side mark in the middle, both mutation-verified. The ruling settles what used to look like an inconsistency: the `loose` path (both slots full) renders `Ht` INBOARD of that side's slots, inside `.msb-slots`, never in `.msb-vs`, so it complies; and an unattributable winner correctly gets NO mark, because "no mark" is the only alternative to a forbidden centre mark. Adding a fifth middle value, or a centre fallback for an unattributable result, is out of bounds. Do not propose either.

A surface that renders the FIK row must show the mark ONLY in that row's centre (operator ruling: the TV header and lobby chips were duplicates and were removed). A surface that renders NO such row (`MatchCard`'s meta strip, the OBS lower-third) keeps its chip, because there the chip IS the one home.

## Where a mark lands

**A middle mark belongs ONLY in the middle of an individual fight** (operator ruling), and a team bout's score is displayed the same way in every format, kachinuki or not: cells either side of a centre, on that bout's row. This governs the WHOLE set (`X`, `(E)`, `(DH)`), i.e. ANY mutating middle, not just one of them.

- In an INDIVIDUAL competition the match IS the fight, so its own row centre carries the mark (`IndividualScore`, whose synthesised sub threads the match-level `decision`/`encho`/`score`). A tied individual match fought on in overtime shows `(E)` there, e.g. `[M][ ] (E) [K][M]`.
- **Encho is NOT team- or kachinuki-specific.** Any tied match may be fought on, in either competition type.
- In a TEAM match the encounter is not itself a fight, so each mark lands on the row of the bout it describes. **Kachinuki** fights on in overtime on the SAME numbered bout, so `(E)` lands in that bout's middle. **Regular (fixed-order)** teams settle a tie with a daihyosen, which is an EXTRA appended bout (position -1, knockout only: `AddDaihyosen` returns `ErrPoolMatch`, a pool tie is simply a draw), so `(DH)` lands in the middle of THAT extra row. A drawn bout carries `X` in its own middle under either format.
- `TeamScoreboard` gates the extra DH row on `showDH && tied`, and `showDH` additionally requires every regular bout to be FINISHED. That is correct, since a daihyosen only follows a completed tied encounter, so a fixture that appends one while a numbered bout is still unscored will (rightly) not render it.
- `TeamScoreboard`'s §277 summary row is an AGGREGATE (IV/PW), not a fight, so its centre is a deliberately empty spacer and NO mark ever goes in it. That holds even when the aggregate itself is tied: a team encounter drawn on equal IV and PW displays no `X` at the encounter level, and if no individual bout was drawn then no `X` appears at all. This is correct because the mark describes a fight, not a standings figure (the draw is carried by the T column and by each drawn bout's own centre). That row RENDERS only on `variant="card"`: on the TV board (`variant="tv"`) it is suppressed and the headline team-name row in display_scoreboard.jsx carries the pairing plus each side's IV/PW instead (operator decision 2026-09-19, bc-lbty -- the team name was being shown twice, and the summary row was the redundant copy). Both surfaces derive those four figures from the one exported `teamIVPWFrom` (match_scoreboard.jsx), so the headline and the row can never disagree about which source won. Do NOT suppress it on the card: viewer_match.jsx has no separate name row, so there that row is the only thing showing the pairing. The headline's IV/PW take their own side's colour (`var(--ink-1)` / `var(--red)`), matching what `.msb-slot`/`.msb-slot--aka` give the row they replace; the headline centre stays the bare `vs`, since IV/PW belong to a SIDE and the centre is a closed set.

Two rejected fixes, both reverted, both verified in the browser: threading the match-level mark into the summary spacer (a review round claimed removing the TV header chip left it homeless) put a second `(E)` directly above the bout's own; and placing the encounter tie there was proposed and refused for the same reason. Do not re-add either. Before acting on any "this mark has nowhere to render" finding, seed the state through the real editor path and check a UI can produce it.

## Hantei is recorded as an ippon

The centre NEVER carries `Ht`: a hantei always names a winner and is decided from a TIED scoreline. `validation.go` requires a winner plus `domain.HanteiTiedScoreline`, the one owner of the tie test, shared with the engine's `preserveSubHantei` exactly as `IsHanteiCompatibleDecision` is, and counted through `domain.CountScoringIppons` so an "•"/empty cell cannot read tied to one enforcer and untied to the other; `hanteiTied` mirrors it in JS. Encho is NOT a precondition. In a TEAM match `validateSubBout` additionally restricts hantei to the daihyosen bout, position -1; a tied numbered bout is simply a hikiwake.

`Ht` IS recorded as an ippon-slice entry (operator ruling 2026-08-21: "Ht should be recorded as just another ippon"). The mark in the WINNER's `IpponsA`/`IpponsB` is the record itself, exactly as the FIK sheet writes it: non-scoring (`IsScoringIppon` drops it, so points/PW/standings/the tie test never count it; `realIppons` is the JS mirror) but first-class in the data. Pool CSV cells and bracket.json ippon arrays hold it verbatim (`domain.ParseScore` still consumes `Ht` as its ONE two-rune token by the no-lone-`t` argument, but only inside the legacy read fold of pre-array bracket files), and the wire carries no `decidedByHantei` field in either direction. The SPA DERIVES that property in `normalizeMatch` for its read sites and `toBackendMatchResult` converts the editors' armed boolean into mark placement (`placeHt`), while the server folds legacy flags into the mark at every read boundary (`state/legacy_hantei.go`; the three `DecidedByHantei` struct fields are LEGACY READ-ONLY, never written).

Placement follows the point rule: the mark fills the winner's next FREE slot in outside-to-inside order (0-0 → the outer slot, 1-1 → the inner one, giving `[K][ ] vs [Ht][M]`), which in array terms is `domain.AppendHantei` (fill a placeholder slot, else append), so the mark's array position IS its slot. Through normal play sanbon-shobu ends at 2, so 0-0 and 1-1 are the only reachable scores and a free slot always exists. 2-2 is IMPOSSIBLE under the rules and closed on both sides: the editors' ippon entry stops each side at 2, and `validateIppons` caps each side at 2 AND rejects 2-2 outright on every sub-bout and on the bulk path. The `loose` return exists solely for hand-edited files. No point is ever overwritten, and each consumer then applies ITS OWN policy: the read-only scoreboard renders the mark beside the slots; both editors drop it because each has a second always-mounted channel for the verdict (see result_slot.jsx's header for the enumeration).

**Which completed bout is the most recent RESULT has ONE owner too**, `web-mobile/js/result_recency.jsx` (`resultRecencyDesc`, a leaf with no imports). Two surfaces answer that question and used to answer it differently (mp-jnvl): the court console's context strip ("the bout just played") and the public viewer's Recent results both read SCHEDULE order as recency, so a court running out of schedule order had them name different bouts. Recency is the `modifiedAt` write stamp, which is the time of the last WRITE and not of the result: STARTING a bout is a stamped write, and an unstamped write inherits the stored stamp, so a started-then-withdrawn bout keeps its start stamp. A bout is unstamped only when nothing ever stamped it (a file predating the field, or a match closed while still `scheduled`), and those fall back to scheduled time, so an all-unstamped list keeps exactly the ordering both surfaces had before. The console applies it in ONE place, `partitionShiaijoMatches`, which orders the COMPLETED group by play time (oldest first) while running and scheduled keep schedule order. That makes the newest result the TAIL, so the strip's anchor and the Completed preview are both plain tails: the preview is a contiguous window and "Show all" EXTENDS the list upward instead of inserting rows into the middle of it (operator ruling 2026-09-19; the earlier top-N-by-recency pick left visible holes). An all-unstamped list falls back to scheduled time and reads exactly as it did before the stamp existed.

The strip's heading names what it is showing whenever that is not the live bout: "Up next" before the court's first bout (the highlight there means "play this next"), "Just played" when it fell back to the last result, "Correcting" when the operator opened that result to fix it. With no such qualifier the lead names the panel's CONTENT - "Standings" for a pool, "Bracket" for a knockout fragment. The word "Context" was removed as meaningless (operator ruling 2026-09-19).

The slot rule has ONE owner, `resultSlot` in web-mobile/js/result_slot.jsx (a small leaf; the dependency reasoning is stated once in that file's header). Its three consumers are `resultCells` in match_scoreboard.jsx, `hanteiSlot`/`ptSlots` in admin_scoring_team.jsx, and the individual editor's slot grid in admin_scoring_individual.jsx (display-only `Ht` chip on the picked side, `hanteiPick`, which adopts `hanteiWinnerKey` from the server). Change the contract and ALL THREE surfaces must be re-checked.

Score strings TRAIL loose marks as an unattributable-winner degradation, but the scoreboard has no centre-`Ht` equivalent: an unattributable winner gets no mark rather than one in the shared centre cell. ACCEPTED no-mark classes (all drifted/legacy data; the centre is forbidden by the operator ruling and no side is attributable): (i) same-name-no-id pairs; (ii) rename-drifted winner names (note the old centre fallback DID fire for these whenever the middle was empty, which a no-encho hantei makes normal, so this IS a regression there, accepted); (iii) winner-less legacy `decidedByHantei` rows (previously centre `Ht`); (iv) untied legacy `decidedByHantei` rows written before the tied-scoreline validation existed (letters still convey the winner; the judges'-decision marker is dropped).

Two related deliberate divergences. Score strings (`sideMarks`) and the Excel export (`SideMarksLR`, over `domain.SideMarks`) still mark `Ht` UNCONDITIONALLY: the mirrored pair stays consistent with itself and gains no tie gate. And a winner naming BOTH sides resolves defensively to AKA everywhere in JS (`subWinnerSides`, aka-first) to match Go's `isWinForSide`/`SideMarksLR` order, so rows, summaries, standings and the export agree on the same arbitrary side. That aka-first rule is scoped to the MATCH level, where a team name is unique by rule and a winner naming both sides is therefore drifted or hand-edited data. It does NOT hold for a SUB-BOUT, where two opposing fighters may legally share a display name and the same order would be a coin flip on ordinary valid data: such a bout is attributed by the row's MEMBER IDS and counts for NEITHER side when no id can decide. A team bout row that names no fighter settles at the match level: `domain.SubBoutAttributionForTeamRow` puts the team names in AFTER that same-name blank, so even a legacy same-named team pair is attributed side A first, as the app and the standings do. **`domain.SubBoutAttribution` owns that rule** and its doc comment is the statement of it; every consumer builds its attribution there, and the JS mirror is `subBoutAttribution` (result_slot.jsx). Do not re-add a name tier that runs when `SideA == SideB`, and do not restate the mechanism here: an earlier copy of it named a function by its unexported name and went stale one commit after that function was exported. Stored sub names stay frozen after a rename (nothing rewrites `SubMatchResult.SideA`/`SideB` on disk or on the wire): every DISPLAY instead resolves the CURRENT name by member id at render time, through `resolveBoutSideDisplayName` (web-mobile/js/lineup_resolver.jsx) and its Go twin `resolveKachinukiDisplayName` (internal/engine/kachinuki_export.go) for the Excel export (bc-dnst), so a rename shows on bouts already fought, on every surface, without touching the stored record. Mirrored helpers: `middleMark`/`enchoLabel` (bracket.jsx) and `sideMarks` (side_marks.jsx, the leaf that also holds `placeMarks`, `winnerSideLR` and `teamMatchMarks`; bracket.jsx re-exports them) ↔ `domain.MiddleMark`/`domain.SideMarks`/`domain.EnchoLabel` (internal/domain/result_marks.go, with `domain.SideMarksAB`, and `domain.IpponsScore` beside IsScoringIppon in ippon.go), moved there (bc-kdsc) so the Kachinuki Detail sheet (internal/helper/excel_kachinuki.go, fed by internal/engine/kachinuki_export.go, which cannot import internal/export without a cycle) reads the SAME closed-set rule rather than restating it. internal/export keeps only what adapts them: `MiddleMark` (from `*state.EnchoMetadata`) and `SideMarksLR` (places the marks White-left). The overtime marker is pinned in both languages via `internal/export/testdata/encho_labels.json` (Go side: `domain.EnchoLabel`).

## Writing `SubResults`

The verdict travels IN the ippons, atomically with the scoreline it rests on: verdict-silence and scoreline-silence are the same thing. `HanteiDecided()` (both result types) reads the mark; `SubMatchResult.DecidedByHantei` is a legacy read-only field that request/load normalization folds away. Never set it.

Every forward replacement of a `SubResults` slice must still call `engine.preserveSubHantei(stored, incoming)` first. It carries a stored daihyosen outcome (winner plus the marked scoreline) onto a scoreline-silent row: the copied prior ippons carry the verdict themselves and no flag is raised. It is deliberately narrow in two directions: it PATCHES an existing position -1 row but never RE-APPENDS a dropped one (`DELETE /daihyosen` removes that row on purpose through this same path), and it refuses any `Decision` outside validateSubBout's allow-list because it runs AFTER validation and its output is never re-checked.

The bracket writes call it via `preserveDaihyosenOutcome` inside **`applyBracketMatchResult`**, the one per-match bracket write shared by `recordBracketMatchResult` and the bronze (3rd-place) fallback. `recordBracketMatchResult` is one body taking an `h state.StoreTx` handle, which `*state.Store` satisfies, so the same `UpdateBracket` dispatch around the shared `applyBracketResultIn` serves the tx and non-tx doors. Do not split it into per-door copies: hand-copied bodies here drifted (the bronze copy missed the LWW guard the round copies had).

Pool writes get it from **`applyPoolWrite` (engine/scoring.go), the single owner of what a stored pool match contributes to an incoming one**. It replaced a block hand-copied into each write closure, after those copies drifted twice in the direction that hurts: the preserve reached three of the writers and missed the live `/score` path, and the `CorrectionReason` inherit reached only the Tx twin, so a team quick-score after a kachinuki reopen blanked the operator's audit justification. It also performs the whole-struct `*stored = *result` overwrite itself, so the overwrite is unreachable without the merge; a new field that must survive it goes there and nowhere else. Note the scope: that covers ENGINE-side preservation, while server-owned flags a client must not be able to clear (`ReopenPending`) are re-stamped at the HTTP boundary in `handlers_match.go` instead.

## Write policy and the rollback snapshot

Both primitives take a `matchWritePolicy`: `matchWriteForward` for a client payload (merged group by group onto the stored match by `mergeMatchWrite`, see "Merge changes, never drop" in `.claude/rules/match-writes.md`; then inherit omitted context, restore a verdict-silent daihyosen), `matchWriteRestore` for the K3 rollback replaying a trusted snapshot (no merge, inherit NOTHING: a nil or empty field means "this was empty", every group and its `GroupStamps` entry is restored exactly, a tombstone included, and a side mismatch is not a client error to reject).

**That policy makes the snapshot PRODUCER load-bearing.** Because an omitted field is read as an instruction to clear, a partial projection is silent data loss, not a missed optimisation. `bracketMatchAsResult` (engine/bracket_result.go) once omitted the score: a bracket match THEN persisted each side as one rendered string while `MatchResult` carries ippon arrays, so a rollback wrote a blank over a real scoreline. bc-bmsc removed that last dual representation. `BracketMatch` now stores the same ippon arrays + hansoku counts as every other result; its `ScoreA`/`ScoreB` strings are LEGACY READ-ONLY (folded into the arrays and cleared by `BracketMatch.NormalizeLegacy`, `state/legacy_hantei.go`, which folds the score strings FIRST and the hantei flag second; arrays win when a hand-edited file carries both; never written, omitempty so the wire never carries them). The projection is a direct field copy, and it carries `ResultSource`/`CorrectionReason` so the restore both puts back the note the match held and clears one the rejected write added (matching `applyPoolWrite`'s whole-struct overwrite, which always did).

It projects `ModifiedAt` and `GroupStamps` too (bc-lww1, bc-mrgc). `mergeMatchWrite` merges nothing under `matchWriteRestore`, before reading any stamp, so a restore can never lose a comparison; what the projected stamps decide is the stamps LEFT behind, the match's real prior times, so a rolled-back match stays ordered against a stale write instead of being left at 0 for `ApplyByTimestamp`'s unstamped bypass. The pool snapshot gets the same from `lookupExistingResult`, which copies the stored `MatchResult` (`copyMatchResults` deep-copies the map and drops the write-only `Changed`/`WriteDoor`/`Merge`). Pinned in `TestBracketRollbackRestoresTheScore` and `TestMerge_RollbackRestoresTheStamps`. `domain.FormatScore` was deleted with the strings; `domain.ParseScore` remains solely as the legacy fold's decoder, pinned parse-direction-only by `TestParseScore_PinnedLegacyBytes` because old files hold those bytes forever.

**The policy must reach BOTH branches.** A match id resolves to a pool or a bracket match at run time and the caller cannot know which, so `writeToPoolOrBracket` threads it through the `errMatchNotFound` fall-through as well. `writeToPoolOrBracket` is ONE body: the store handle `h state.StoreTx` (which `*state.Store` satisfies) is how a caller says whether the per-comp lock is already held, and the non-tx entry points `RecordMatchResult`/`RecordMatchResultWithIneligibility` are `WithTransaction` shims. Never compensate for one branch in a caller (e.g. by pre-converting the snapshot's nils into explicit clears): that makes one function enumerate another's nil-preserve fields, and the next such field silently breaks rollback. The policy differences are pinned by mutation in `pool_write_merge_test.go`, `sub_hantei_preserve_test.go` (`TestBracketRollbackDoesNotReapplyTheWrite`) and the pair `TestRollback_BracketSubResults_Cleared`/`_ClearedTx`, which pins both entry points into the one rollback body.

## Editors adopt the server's verdict

**A match has ONE result and every surface asking for it shows the same one** (operator ruling), and the score editors are such surfaces. While one is open, the viewer card, the bracket, the TV board, the lobby and the export are all already showing whatever the server holds, so an editor showing something else is a divergence whatever its reason. Both editors therefore ADOPT a verdict recorded on another device: `useEffect` keyed on the VALUE (`hanteiRecorded` in admin_scoring_individual.jsx; `daihyosenHanteiRecorded` + `recordedDaihyosenSide` in admin_scoring_team.jsx), never on the `m`/`match` object, which SSE re-creates on every broadcast and which would fight the operator's every tap. Keying on the primitive means a local arm stands (it does not move the server value), and a team daihyosen pick or withdrawal, which is saved as it is made, is kept until the server holds it (`keepLocalEdits`), while a real server change is followed in either direction, and only the verdict is touched, so an edit in progress survives. The individual editor's hantei adopt stays; its running SCORE needs no adopt at all (bc-evts stage 3): on a running match its board is the server's match with this device's pending taps folded on (`tapBoardOf`: the newer of the host's match and the last score-events answer, `latestDerivedMatch`, by `isOlderRunningCopy`, then `applyPending`), so another device's point shows as soon as it arrives and this device's own taps stay on it until the server holds them. `isDirty` compares the verdict against the SERVER's value, not a mount-time snapshot, so adopting is not an "unsaved change" and does not fire the discard prompt on an untouched editor.

Because an editor can now never rule on something it did not display, an explicit `false` is always the operator's decision and always reaches the wire as what it now IS, a markless scoreline: `daihyosenEnchoFields` still states the local boolean unconditionally, and the serializer chokepoint converts it (true → mark placed on the winner's side, false → arrays stay markless; `realIppons` strips echoed marks first so placement never doubles). Two earlier designs are recorded in `web-mobile/js/__tests__/render/individual_editor_live_hantei.render.test.jsx` and must not be reintroduced: recomputing the baseline from the live prop while freezing the local armed state (which erased the verdict on the next write), and freezing at mount with a `hanteiKnown` silence guard (which stopped the erase by accepting the divergence; the guard was deleted with it).

**The edit-time adopt rule.** A score editor holds its board against a server copy stamped before the operator's last unsent edit, because comparing values alone reads a tap taken back during a save as untouched, and that save's echo then wipes it. Its two owners are the team sheet's `rowTakenFromServer`/`repRowTakes` (bc-kclr) and the engi editor's adopt (`flagsEditedAtRef`, bc-evts), until stages 4 and 5 move those editors to taps. The individual editor's edit-time stamp (bc-ltap) is retired with its autosave: its running board is the server's match plus its pending taps (see above), so `adoptServerScore` there takes nothing on a running match, only agreeing the `points` and `encho` groups (`claim.agree`) while no tap of that group is pending, and applies the server's score (`applyServerScore`) only off running. `landedAutosave` (admin_scoring_autosave.jsx) clears a stamp once a save carrying the last edit has landed, judged on the outbox's SETTLED answer (`settled.then`, a callback, never a wait: the pending answer the outbox gives at once says nothing about the server), so every later server copy is followed. Both editors take the stamp only while the match is running, because only then is the edit autosaved: on a scheduled match or a correction of a completed one nothing is sent, no landed save would ever clear the stamp, and a net-zero fiddle would make the editor refuse another device's copy it would otherwise have adopted. Both also clear it when the match leaves running (an effect keyed on the status): another device finishing the match or sending it back to the queue inside the debounce makes `fireRunningWrite` drop the write with no callback, so no landed save would clear it. A REFUSED autosave (`writeWasRefused`: nothing handed back, or `applied:false`) stores and broadcasts nothing, so no later copy would re-run the adopt and the stamp would hold the operator's value against the server's for good. `landedAutosave` therefore takes an optional `onRefused(seq)`; each editor, when no edit was made since that save was built (the `seq` still matches), clears the stamp and takes the server's CURRENT copy through its own adopt path (the engi counts, the team sheet's rows and pick parts the save carried, composed by `composeRepRow`), reading the latest render's closure through a ref. An edit made since keeps the stamp and the save that carries it decides; the engi editor also requires the stamp still to be set, because their counter counts only running edits, so a correction tapped on a match that finished elsewhere while the save was out is not wiped by a late refusal. A QUEUED answer calls neither callback and keeps the stamp: that write will land. A PENDING answer (every autosave is the outbox's since stage 3) calls them when its `settled` answer comes; a rejection there is a refusal. A `clock_skew` answer (`writeWasRefusedForClock`) calls neither callback either and keeps the stamp: the outbox relearns the clock and re-stamps and resends the entry once (`_restampFor`), and one refused twice leaves the tap on the board for the NEXT save to carry with a corrected stamp. Known limits: the team sheet's refusal adopt (`takeServerRowsRef`) restores bout rows and representative picks only, so the representative-bout verdict and the encounter overtime stay local after a refused save; and a save applied IN PART (`heldGroups`) counts as landed and clears the stamp, and no new broadcast follows when the server's value for a held group equals the copy the adopt already skipped; that stays the genuine disagreement `keepLocalEdits` leaves to the operator, with the held values in the match's history.

## Silence on the wire

Silence still means "no opinion" on the wire. It is now OMITTED IPPONS (nil, not `[]`) rather than an omitted flag, and the distinction is load-bearing: an explicit `[]` is the operator saying ZERO (a 0-0 withdrawal), a missing key is a writer with nothing to say, whose stored verdict `preserveSubHantei` restores. The team editor IS one of those writers: `buildPatch` (admin_scoring_team.jsx) has a `daihyosenSilent` branch that OMITS the daihyosen row's ippon arrays when the operator has not touched that row this session AND nothing about it is known locally. That branch is the only reason the nil case is reachable from a real client at all, so do NOT "fix" it into an unconditional write. Any touch, or any locally-known verdict or scoreline, falls through to the explicit-array path, so a deliberate withdrawal still withdraws. Scope note: the restore protects a stored HANTEI verdict only; `preserveSubHantei` bails when the stored row carries no mark, so a rep bout decided by a strike is not restored by this mechanism.

Accepted compat gap (documented in `legacy_hantei.go`): a pre-ruling client re-scoring a hantei match with markless ippons replayed from the offline queue (which has no age limit since bc-mrgc phase 3) clears the verdict when its stamp still orders it after the stored one, because a markless scoreline is now the writer's word.

## A write answers for what it introduces, not for what it inherited

The engine-side contradiction guard is `stripInvalidHantei` (replaced `preserveMatchHantei`): forward writes on both branches strip a mark whose preconditions fail (`hanteiStillHolds`) or that sits on a non-winner's side, and LOG the drop (it discards an operator-visible verdict on an otherwise successful write). It strips rather than rejecting BECAUSE the mark is one the write INHERITED: the reachable producer is `handlers_daihyosen.go`'s add path, which copies the stored match wholesale (`u := *match`) and moves Status to running, so a valid stored verdict fails `hanteiStillHolds` on status alone and a 400 would blame the operator for state already on disk (same rule as `applyHansokuIppons`' fold scoping; pinned by `TestStripInvalidHantei_InheritedMarkIsStrippedNotRejected`). A CLIENT-sent bad mark is rejected instead, at the boundary that can still tell whose fault it is (`mobileapp.validateHanteiMarkPlacement`, which the handler's `backfillMatchIdentityForHantei` feeds the stored sides/ids so it attributes by the same triple the engine will). The decision twins are NOT a producer: `preserveLoserScore` filters through `struckIppons` and `domain.IsScoringIppon` drops the mark, so a kiken over a stored hantei arrives with the mark already gone (`TestPreserveLoserScoreDropsTheHanteiMark`).

`applyHansokuIppons` folds each row and immediately judges what THAT fold rewrote. It is count-compared: a row whose award is already reflected is left untouched, slots and all, and reports NO change; a shortfall is awarded through `domain.AppendIppon`, filling a free `•` slot before growing the slice, so a wire-legal placeholder row stays wire-legal after the award; a surplus H over the count is the operator's own strike (the editors keep hansoku as the OUTSTANDING count and land the H themselves, `applyFoulIncrement`) and is NEVER removed. It applies both checks, hantei-untied and the best-of-3 overflow, at match level and sub level through the one shared `checkFoldedRow`. The check lives inside the producer rather than in a separate function the call sites chain by hand, so a new write path cannot fold without checking. Unscoped, it rejected rows the decision twins had just inherited wholesale via `preserveLoserScore`, so an over-cap row already on disk 400'd an operator's kiken that carried no sub rows at all, with no endpoint able to repair it; unmirrored at match level, the identical fold wedged a match on disk instead. This narrows the engine guard on purpose: a payload that ALREADY carries a bad row is damage `mobileapp.validateIppons` can see and rejects at both levels, so only the post-award state needs checking here. `applyKachinukiMerge` running AFTER the check is the same rule; when you add a guard downstream of an inherit, ask which actor it will blame.
