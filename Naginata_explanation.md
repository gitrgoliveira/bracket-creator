# Naginata tournament differences
When comparing with a kendo tournament, a Naginata competition has the following differences:

 - There's one more scoring target called Sune
 - There's no joint 3rd place. There's an extra match to decide who is 3rd or 4th, before the final.
 - Only 1, 2 and 3 positions get medals
 - Engi-Kyogi is a special type of Kata competition that is scored based on the number of flags raised by the referees
 

## Naginata Engi-kyogi (Kata & Shikake-Oji) Competition Guide

### Overview of Engi-kyogi
**Engi-kyogi (演技競技)** is a choreographed demonstration of set forms or techniques in Naginata. Unlike *Shiai-kyogi* (the combat match format where participants wear protective armor/*bogu* and fight to score points), Engi-kyogi matches involve teams of two competitors executing pre-designated forms against another pair. It is designed to encourage the correct diffusion, development, technical precision, and aesthetic beauty of Naginata.

Competitors perform in pairs where one is the initiator/attacker (**Shikake**) and the other is the defender/counter-attacker (**Oji**). The match is judged on a comparative or point-deduction basis, emphasizing precision, attitude, posture, and partner harmony.

---

### Variations of Engi-kyogi
According to standard regulations, there are two primary disciplines under Engi-kyogi:

#### 1. Shikake-Oji (しかけ応じ / 仕掛応じ)
* **Equipment:** Competitors use standard match naginata (flexible bamboo blade/*habu* and oak shaft/*ebu*) identical to those used in bogu training and shiai.
* **Format:** A set number of prearranged technical sequences are performed in pairs. 
* **Forms:** There are **8 Shikake-Oji sequences** in total.
  * **Official Championships** (e.g., European Championships): Pairs compete on **Ippon-me, Yonhon-me, and Hachihon-me (1, 4, 8)**.
  * **Goodwill / Lower Rank Competitions:** Pairs compete on **Nihon-me and Sanbon-me (2, 3)**.

#### 2. All Japan Naginata Federation Kata (全日本なぎなた形)
* **Equipment:** Competitors utilize a solid wooden naginata (*kata-naginata*), which features realistic curves and corners (*mune*, *shinogi*, etc.) and is similar in function to the wooden sword (*bokuto*) in Kendo. 
* **Format:** Sophisticated choreographies emphasizing traditional battlefield principles. Due to the intricacy and weight of the wooden weapons, Kata is generally restricted to practitioners of rank **3rd Dan and above**.
* **Forms:** Consists of **7 set forms**.
  * **Official Championships** (e.g., European Championships): Pairs compete on **Ippon-me, Sanbon-me, and Gohon-me (1, 3, 5)**.

---

### Adjudication & Victory Criteria (Flag Decision Method / *Hata-keishiki*)
The Flag Decision Method (*Hata-keishiki* / 旗形式) is the standard adjudication system for official championships, including the European Championships. Adjudication is comparative, decided by a panel of referees raising colored flags to designate the superior pair.

* **Referee Cohort:** The referee panel can consist of **1, 3, or 5 referees** (typically 5 in major championships, comprising one *Shushin* / Head Referee and four *Fukushin* / Sub-Referees).
* **Procedure:** 
  1. Two pairs, one wearing red ribbons (*Aka*) and the other white (*Shiro*), enter the court and bow.
  2. On the Shushin's whistle, both pairs perform the designated forms simultaneously.
  3. After completing the forms and marching off the court, the Shushin blows a whistle.
  4. The referees raise either a red or white flag to indicate their choice of the superior pair/color.
  5. The winner is decided by a majority of the flags raised for either side/color (e.g., minimum 3 out of 5 flags, or 2 out of 3 flags).
* **Pool Play Standings (Engi-kyogi only):** In Engi-kyogi round-robin pools, victory and rankings are determined sequentially by:
  1. **Total Number of Wins:** The pair with the most overall match victories ranks higher.
  2. **Total Number of Flags:** If wins are tied, the pair with the highest number of accumulated individual flags for their side/color (e.g., a 5-0 win yields 5 flags, a 3-2 win yields 3 flags) ranks higher.

> [!NOTE]
> **Point Method (*Saiten-keishiki* / 採点形式):** While point scoring (judging matches out of 10 points with deductions) exists in the regulations, it is rarely used in official championships. The core principles of fault evaluation under Saiten-keishiki are instead used by referees as a mental guideline to weigh the severity of mistakes in Hata-keishiki matches.
